The original 227 breeding guides cover the Society's points list. Every other species can get one too, written to the same standard: the AI writes the guide, then a separate pass fact-checks it claim by claim (how it breeds, sexing, every number, first foods, whether the parents eat the young) and fixes what it can. Nothing goes live until you say yes.

## When does a draft get written?
- **Automatically** when you add a new species from a request with **Save to library and reward**. The draft takes about 2 minutes and appears on **Breeding guides** in the admin menu, with a badge.
- **On demand**: the **Draft a guide for a species** box at the bottom of the page lists every species without a guide of its own. Pick one and press **Draft a guide**.

Each draft costs roughly 20 to 40 cents. The card shows the actual cost.

Varieties (color and fin forms like Electric Blue Ram) don't get their own guide. Their species page shows the parent's guide, with a line saying it's bred the same way.

## How do I review a draft?
Each card shows **What the fact-check found**: how confident it is, what it fixed, and anything worth confirming. Under that is the whole guide, with every part editable: summary, intro, the at-a-glance facts, each section and the FAQ answers. Text like {{temp}} or {{ph}} fills in from the species page, so the guide always matches it.

Then answer:
- **Yes, publish it** puts it live, with your edits, on the breeding guides and on the species page.
- **No** throws the draft out. You can draft it again later.
- **Something else** rewrites it with your note ("breeders say it needs softer water", "make the fry section simpler") and fact-checks it again.

## How do these guides differ from the Society ones?
A guide for a species that isn't on the Society's points list has no class, points or certificate. On the breeding guides page it's listed under **More species**, after the classes. If the species is on the Society list, the draft also gets a spawn log tip and the certificate shows as usual.

## Where do the facts come from?
Both passes search the web first (breeders' reports, specialist societies, care sheets and papers), like the research behind the original guides, and the checker verifies against what it finds. It's told to say when sources disagree or a species is rarely bred, and to give ranges rather than made-up precise numbers. Read the "Worth confirming" list before you publish.
